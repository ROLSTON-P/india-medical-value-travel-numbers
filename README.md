# India medical value travel numbers, with sources

Every public figure this site cites on medical travel to India, each with its source, its date and a note.

Last checked 24 October 2026. Updated quarterly. The same table, with its notes, is on [rolstonpaul.com](https://rolstonpaul.com/guides/india-medical-value-travel-numbers).

## What this dataset is

This dataset keeps the public numbers on medical value travel to India that this site cites. Medical value travel is the term India's government and industry use for travel to another country for treatment. Each number sits in a row with what it measures, the period it covers, its source, the date the source was published and a note.

Every number comes from a published source that the site has read and keeps a copy of. Nothing here is a forecast. This site has not worked out any total or percentage of its own. Where two sources give different numbers for the same thing, both rows are shown and both are marked disputed.

The numbers were last checked on 24 October 2026. It will be checked again every quarter, which means every three months, and the date will change when it is.

## Why the numbers matter

Numbers about medical travel to India are quoted often and sourced rarely. A figure copied from a copy loses its date, its period and what it counted. A reader then cannot tell a count of patients from a count of visas.

India's government reports these arrivals to Parliament. The counts come from the Bureau of Immigration, the agency that records who enters the country. That is a public record a reader abroad can check, and India's case is stronger when it rests on numbers like that.

The same record has limits. The Ministry of Tourism told the Lok Sabha that it keeps no state-wise data on arrivals by purpose and no data on revenue from medical tourism. So there is no official count of patients by state, and every revenue figure here is an estimate.

The numbers also show a risk. Business Today reported in March 2025 that Bangladesh accounts for nearly 70% of India's medical value travel. When one country sends most of the patients, one border decision can move the national total.

## How to read and cite these numbers

- Quote a number with its source and its period. For example: over 6.59 lakh foreign tourists came to India for medical treatment in 2023, from Bureau of Immigration data reported by DD News.
- One lakh is one hundred thousand. FY25 means India's financial year from April 2024 to March 2025.
- Read the note on a row before you use it. Some rows count patients, some count visas and some count every visitor from one country.
- A visa is not an arrival. Factly notes that the Ministry of External Affairs reports medical visas, while the Ministry of Tourism counts arrivals, not people, so a patient who comes twice is counted twice.
- A row marked disputed has a second row with a different number for the same thing. Give both numbers with their sources, or wait for the primary release.
- Link to the original source where you can, and to the page on rolstonpaul.com for the comparison.

There is a trade-off here. One clean number is easier to quote than two that disagree. The ministry's own table, published by PIB on 9 February 2026, gives 6,44,387 for 2024, the same as DD News. In my judgement that is the figure to lead with, with Business Standard's 625,000 named beside it until the gap is explained.

## Where the sources disagree

- The 2024 total. The ministry's table published by PIB gives 6,44,387 foreign tourist arrivals for medical purposes, and DD News reports the same as 6.44 lakh. Business Standard reports 625,000. All three cite Bureau of Immigration data. For 2023 all three agree. The rows stay marked disputed until the gap is explained.
- Bangladesh's share. Business Today gives nearly 70% of medical value travel, with no period. Business Today, citing ICRIER, gives 69% of medical tourists in 2022. Business Standard, citing ICRA, gives 70 to 75 per cent of medical visas. These measure different things, so they do not contradict each other, but they should not be swapped.
- Visas and arrivals. Factly's visa counts and the ministry's arrival counts come from different offices and count different things. A year's visas and that year's arrivals should never be compared as if one were a share of the other.
- Visitors from Bangladesh in late 2024. Business Today's monthly falls are for all visitors from Bangladesh. The article does not say how many of them came for treatment.
